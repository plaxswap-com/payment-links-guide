Crypto payment links: how they work and when to use them

A short guide for freelancers, small sellers and anyone who sends invoices. Updated November 2026.

A crypto payment link is a web address that asks for a set amount of one coin. You send the link, or its QR code, to the person who owes you money. They open it, see exactly what to send, and pay from their own wallet or exchange. You do not need a website, a shop plugin or a checkout page of your own.

When a payment link is the right tool

  • Invoices. A client owes you 250 USDT for a piece of work. One link with the amount and a title such as "Invoice 0142" is easier than pasting an address and hoping they pick the right network.
  • Selling without a website. If you sell through a chat group or social media, a link or QR code takes the place of a checkout.
  • A fixed price paid many times. Some services let a link accept repeat payments of the same amount, which suits a fixed fee such as a course seat or a monthly service.

Amount and coin choice

The most important setting is the amount and the coin you want to receive. Stablecoins such as USDT or USDC keep the value close to the dollar. Bitcoin or Ether links carry price movement until you swap or spend them.

The network matters as much as the coin. USDT exists on TRON, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Solana and other networks, and each network uses a different address format. A good link fixes the network so the payer cannot send to the wrong one.

Expiry: how long a link stays open

Most links have an end date. A short window, such as one hour, suits a sale where the price may change. A longer one, such as seven or thirty days, suits an invoice the client will pay when they get to it. Separately, the price a payer sees is usually fixed for a few minutes only. Once they continue, they get a set time to send, and if they miss it they simply start again.

Partial payments and overpayments

People sometimes send too little, often because their exchange took a withdrawal fee out of the amount. Before you use a service, check three things: what happens to a short payment, where an overpayment goes, and where the coins go if a payment fails.

Quick checklist

  • Pick a stablecoin if you want a predictable value.
  • Fix the network and double check your receiving address.
  • Choose an expiry that matches how fast you expect to be paid.
  • Save any manage or admin link the service gives you.
